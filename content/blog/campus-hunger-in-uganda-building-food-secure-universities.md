# Campus Hunger in Uganda: Building Food-Secure Universities

Campus hunger and food insecurity are realities facing university students in Uganda, yet they remain largely absent from conversations about higher education, student wellbeing and academic success.

For some students, campus food insecurity means skipping meals because they cannot afford them. For others, it means eating less than they need, relying on the cheapest available food, struggling to access nutritious meals or spending an entire day in class without eating.

These experiences raise an important question for Uganda's higher education system:

> **What does a food-secure university look like, and what will it take to build one?**

On **21 October 2026**, Learn And Lunch, together with the Kampala International University Guild Union, will convene the **Campus Food Security Summit 2026** at Kampala International University in Kampala, Uganda.

Under the theme **“Building Food-Secure Universities in Uganda,”** the Summit will bring students into conversation with universities, government, researchers, nutrition and food-system professionals, civil society, development partners and the private sector.

The goal is simple: **make campus hunger visible, understand the problem better and begin moving from conversation towards action.**

## What Is Campus Hunger?

Campus hunger describes the experience of university and college students who struggle to consistently access enough food to meet their needs while pursuing their education.

The wider issue is **campus food insecurity**.

Food insecurity among university students is not simply about having nothing to eat. Students may experience different levels of difficulty accessing food that is adequate, affordable, nutritious and safe.

This can include:

* Skipping meals because of financial difficulties
* Going through lectures or entire days without eating
* Reducing meal portions to make limited money last longer
* Depending primarily on inexpensive foods regardless of nutritional value
* Having limited access to affordable and nutritious food around campus
* Facing concerns about food quality, hygiene or safety
* Struggling to balance food expenses with tuition, accommodation, transport and other university costs

These experiences can easily remain invisible because students often manage them privately.

That is one reason **Learn And Lunch is working to bring campus hunger into Uganda's wider conversation about student wellbeing and success.**

## Why Campus Hunger in Uganda Deserves Attention

University students are often treated as independent adults who should be able to manage their own food needs.

But entering university does not make vulnerability disappear.

Students are navigating tuition, accommodation, transport, academic costs and everyday living expenses, often with limited financial resources. Food can become one of the expenses they compromise when those pressures increase.

This matters because university should provide young people with an environment in which they can learn, participate and develop.

A student struggling to consistently access food is not experiencing a problem separate from education.

**Food security is part of the environment that makes education possible.**

That is why Learn And Lunch believes campus hunger should be understood not only as a food issue, but as an issue connected to **student wellbeing, health, dignity, equality and educational success.**

## What Is a Food-Secure University?

A food-secure university is not simply a campus where food is sold.

It is a university environment where students can consistently access food that supports their wellbeing and enables them to participate meaningfully in university life.

Building food-secure universities requires us to ask difficult questions.

Can students afford the food available around campus?

Are nutritious options accessible?

What happens when a student cannot afford a meal?

What do university food environments encourage students to eat?

What responsibilities should universities have?

What role should government play?

How can student leaders, food providers, researchers, civil society and the private sector contribute?

And most importantly:

**What are students themselves experiencing?**

These questions are at the centre of the Campus Food Security Summit 2026.

## Putting Student Voices at the Centre

Campus hunger cannot be understood only through institutions, experts and statistics.

We need to listen to students.

Students experiencing food insecurity understand what it looks like to attend lectures hungry, compromise meals because of cost, navigate campus food environments and make difficult choices between competing expenses.

The Campus Food Security Summit therefore creates space for students to speak about their experiences, priorities and ideas.

Students should not simply be beneficiaries of solutions created for them.

They should participate in defining the problem and shaping the response.

Our approach is:

> ### STUDENT VOICES → EVIDENCE → DIALOGUE → COMMITMENTS → ACTION

Student experiences help us understand the problem.

Evidence helps us understand its scale, causes and consequences.

Dialogue brings students together with institutions capable of responding.

Commitments establish what different actors are willing to do.

Follow-up helps turn those commitments into action.

## Campus Hunger Requires More Than One Solution

There is unlikely to be one intervention that solves university student food insecurity.

Different students and universities experience different challenges.

Responses could require attention to affordability, nutrition, university food environments, student welfare, research, food systems, financial pressures, institutional policy, partnerships and other factors.

That is precisely why a multi-sector conversation matters.

Learn And Lunch does not believe one organisation can solve campus hunger alone.

Our role is to help **make the issue visible, strengthen student voice, build evidence, bring responsible institutions into the conversation and support movement towards practical action.**

## Beyond the Campus Food Security Summit

The Summit is a starting point, not the end.

Beyond 21 October, Learn And Lunch intends to continue working with students, Guilds, student clubs and associations, universities, researchers, government and other partners to deepen understanding of campus food insecurity and advocate for practical responses.

We want to see stronger student participation in decisions affecting campus food environments.

We want more evidence about university student food insecurity in Uganda.

We want universities and other institutions to increasingly recognise campus food security as part of student wellbeing and success.

And we want the commitments and partnerships emerging from the Summit to lead somewhere.

**The bigger goal is to move campus hunger from an invisible student experience to an issue that institutions recognise, understand and act upon.**
