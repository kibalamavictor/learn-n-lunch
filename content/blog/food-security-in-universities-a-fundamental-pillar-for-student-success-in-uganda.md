When we think about student success, we often focus on quality education, scholarships, and modern learning environments. Yet one essential factor is frequently overlooked: **food security**.

Across Uganda, many university students struggle with **food insecurity**, skipping meals or worrying about where their next meal will come from. This silent challenge affects far more than physical health. It affects learning, wellbeing, and academic success.

If universities are committed to helping students thrive, then food security must become part of the conversation.

## Why Food Security Matters

Food security means having reliable access to sufficient, safe, nutritious, and affordable food.

For university students, this is more than meeting a basic need. Good nutrition supports concentration, memory, energy, and overall wellbeing, all of which are essential for learning.

When students experience **campus hunger**, they are more likely to struggle academically, experience stress, and disengage from university life.

## Campus Hunger Is an Education Issue

Too often, **food insecurity among university students** is viewed as a personal or financial problem. In reality, it is an educational challenge.

Students cannot fully benefit from lectures, libraries, or academic support if hunger limits their ability to focus and perform. Food security should therefore be recognized as an essential pillar of student success, not simply a welfare concern.

## Moving From Awareness to Action

Addressing campus hunger requires more than temporary relief.

Universities, students, researchers, and policymakers must work together to understand the causes of food insecurity and develop sustainable solutions that strengthen student wellbeing and academic outcomes.

Ending campus hunger is not about charity. It is about creating learning environments where every student has an equal opportunity to succeed.

## The Learn And Lunch Approach

At **Learn And Lunch**, we believe that **ending campus hunger is fundamental to unlocking student success**.

Through research, advocacy, nutrition education, and solution design, we are working to make **food security in universities** a priority in Uganda's higher education system.

Our goal is not to blame institutions, but to encourage a new way of thinking, one that recognizes food security as a strategic investment in student success.

## Building Food-Secure Universities

A university cannot achieve its full potential if its students are learning on empty stomachs.

Creating **food-secure universities in Uganda** means investing in healthier, more productive, and more successful students. It means recognizing that food is not separate from education. It is one of the foundations upon which meaningful learning is built.

As conversations about higher education continue to evolve, food security deserves a place at the centre of student success.
